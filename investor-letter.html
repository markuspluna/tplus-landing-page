We also spent a lot of time figuring out our market microstructure. t+ is fairly unique as a decentralized brokerage/CEX:

  • We treat a large number of like-assets as fungible
  • We support cross margining with external venues
  • We're atomically composable with external chains
  • We allow margin and spot traders to trade in the same book but treat margin positions similarly to perp positions
  • We use unique fee and order models to allow market participants that specialize in different strategies to benefit from their specialization

All of this was done to drive down costs and improve capital efficiency. But it creates new problems like:

  • "How do we control exposure skew?"
  • "How do we ensure fungible assets are evenly distributed across chains?"
  • "How do we allow margin and spot traders to trade in the same book but treat margin positions similarly to perp positions?"
  • "How do we use unique fee and order models to allow market participants that specialize in different strategies to benefit from their specialization?"
  • And many more...

These challenges are solved now - and you'll hear a lot more about them in the future as we start shouting about them.

Are we worried about the broader market?

It's no secret that people are generally scared and bearish right now for a variety of reasons. As a result we get a lot of questions about whether we're worried it's a bad environment to launch a trading product in.

Our answer is: "we don't really care about the broader market"

First off - t+ supports literally every asset under the sun without having to source liquidity or inventory for them. Wherever there's speculative appetite, we serve it. As a result, we're much less exposed to market trends than incumbent platforms.

That being said, as a trading platform t+ is still sensitive to overall speculative flow (or a lack thereof). But the neat part of t+ is that it's valuable as a market utility as well as a platform. At a baseline, without any UI, t+ is a capital efficiency tool for algorithmic traders. To provide some examples:

  • Solvers can use us to atomically access external liquidity sources (CEX's or other chains) without having to manage a book or execution in those venues. Just examining cowswap mainnet - an average of $89 million is filled per day by PMM's. We can capture a lot of that just by providing a more capital efficient, competitive, alternative to a PMM (which will thus have better quotes).
  • CEX<>DEX arbitrageurs can use us to reduce their capital cost and non-execution risk. This represents $70m+ in average daily volume just considering ETH<>USD liquidity on Arbitrum and mainnet.

Furthermore, as perp platforms proliferate and equity perps take off we have even more markets of slightly different of like assets to take advantage of pricing inefficiencies in for the benefit of our users.
